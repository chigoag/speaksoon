How often should you actually contact your close friends?

12 June 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: there's no magic number — but for the handful of people closest to you, a light touch every week or two is usually enough to keep the bond warm. What matters far more than frequency is that contact happens at all, and that it feels genuine rather than dutiful.

Is there a "right" frequency?

Not a universal one. The friendships in your life run at different speeds. A best friend from childhood can go quiet for a month and pick straight back up; a newer friendship needs more regular tending to take root. So the honest answer is: it depends on the person — and that's freeing, not frustrating.

What the research hints at

Psychologist Robin Dunbar's work suggests we can only really sustain around 150 relationships, with a close inner circle of about five — and those inner-circle bonds need the most frequent contact to stay strong. Separately, research by Jeffrey Hall found it takes roughly 200 hours together to become close friends. The pattern is clear: closeness is built and kept through accumulated small moments, not the occasional grand gesture.

Match the rhythm to the person

Rather than one rule for everyone, give each close friend their own cadence — the rough interval that feels right for that relationship. Weekly for the people you speak to most; monthly for the old friend you can always pick up with. Once you stop holding everyone to the same schedule, staying in touch feels a lot lighter.

Quality beats frequency — and a quick check-in counts

You don't need a long phone call to keep a friendship alive. A two-line "thinking of you," a meme that's so them, a "how did the interview go?" — small, sincere touches carry a relationship between the bigger catch-ups. Don't wait for the perfect moment; the small ones add up.

Let go of the guilt

If it's been too long, the fix isn't to feel bad — it's to reach out now. Friendship isn't a scoreboard, and nobody's counting. The people who matter would far rather hear from you late than not at all.

FAQ

How often should you text a close friend?
There's no fixed rule, but a light check-in every week or two keeps most close friendships feeling connected.

Is it normal to go months without speaking to a good friend?
Yes — deep friendships can survive long gaps. But the longer the silence, the more intentional you have to be about breaking it.

How do I keep in touch when life gets busy?
Lower the bar. A quick message counts, and setting a gentle reminder for the people who matter beats relying on memory.
