How to rekindle an old friendship

12 June 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: just reach out. A short, warm, no-pressure message — one that names the gap honestly and says why they came to mind — is almost always all it takes. The reunion you're nervous about is usually one the other person has been quietly hoping for too.

The fear that stops us

We don't avoid reaching out because we don't care. We avoid it because we've built it up: it's been too long, they'll think it's strange, what if it's awkward? But that fear is almost always bigger in your head than in reality. Most people are genuinely touched to hear from someone who mattered to them — being remembered feels good.

What to actually say

Keep it simple, warm, and specific. You don't need a grand explanation for the silence. Try something like:

"Hi! You popped into my head today — I drove past our old café and thought of you. It's been way too long. How have you been?"

A concrete reason (a memory, a song, a place) makes it feel real rather than random.

Don't over-apologise for the gap

Resist the urge to grovel about how long it's been. A long, guilt-laden apology puts pressure on the other person and makes the reunion heavier than it needs to be. A light "it's been far too long!" does the job and keeps the mood warm.

Offer something small and specific

If it flows naturally, suggest a low-stakes next step — a phone call this week, a coffee if you're nearby, a "we have to catch up properly soon." Specific and small beats a vague "we should hang out" that never happens.

Then keep it going

The hard part isn't the first message — it's not letting the friendship drift again. Once you've reconnected, give it a gentle rhythm so the next gap doesn't stretch into years. Rekindling is lovely; keeping the flame lit is the real win.

FAQ

How do you reconnect with an old friend after years?
Send a short, warm message that names the gap honestly and shares why they came to mind. Keep it low-pressure — you're saying hello, not booking a holiday.

What do you say to an old friend you haven't spoken to in years?
Something simple and genuine: "You popped into my head today and I realised it's been far too long. How are you?" A specific memory makes it land even better.

Is it weird to message a friend you've lost touch with?
Almost never. Most people are delighted to hear from someone who mattered to them — the awkwardness you imagine is far bigger than what they'll feel.
